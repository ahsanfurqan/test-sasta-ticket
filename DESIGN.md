# DESIGN

> **Stub.** This is the document the assessment says is read most carefully after the code.
> It is written as the system is built, from the ADRs in `docs/adr/` — not reconstructed
> from memory at the end, which is how alternatives get lost and every decision starts to
> look inevitable.
>
> Session 1 built the skeleton: context, ownership, decision process, and a stack that
> boots. The sections below are the outline the brief asks for, with what exists so far.

## The pieces, what each one owns, and where the boundaries are

Five packages, one owning agent each (`.claude/agents/`), documented in `CLAUDE.md`.
The load-bearing boundary is `meter.domain`: pure functions over integers, importing
nothing from the API, storage or pipeline layers and nothing that performs I/O, enforced
mechanically by import-linter. Everything else in the design follows from being able to
rate a charge without touching infrastructure.

## The data model, concretely

*Not yet written.* Blocked on open question #7 — whether custom pricing is an override on
a plan or a price list of its own — because that choice changes the shape of the plan and
price tables, and those tables are referenced by invoices that must never change.

Settled so far: money is `bigint` paisa (ADR-0004); SQLAlchemy ORM models are schema truth
with Alembic migrations, autogenerated as a draft and finished by hand (ADR-0003).

## How usage gets from a served request into something billable, and what happens if that path fails partway

*Not yet built.* The tension is stated in `CLAUDE.md`: the hot path must acknowledge the
customer before the usage is durable, which creates a window where a crash loses revenue.
The design owes: how large that window is, how it is detected, and how it is reconciled.

## How the live usage figure and the exact invoice are reconciled

*Not yet built.* Both must derive from the same events, with a stated lag for the live
figure and no tolerance at all for the invoice.

## How the spending limit works, and the longest a customer could go over it

*Not yet built.* Open question #3 — this needs to be answered as a number, not a mechanism,
and the number is what the enforcement design gets built against.

## What happens on a mid-month plan change, and how we explain that charge

*Not yet decided.* Open question #1, with four candidate policies and the customer-facing
sentence each one produces. Commercial handed this back explicitly.

## What we left out of a first version, and why that was right

*In progress.* Session 1 deliberately shipped no billing logic: the point was to get the
seams, the ownership and the decision record in place first, so that the pricing math lands
in a package that cannot reach for a database.

## Where this design starts to struggle as traffic grows, and roughly at what point

*Partly answered.* Each ADR carries a "where it breaks" section; ADR-0003 names the usage
table's bulk-insert path as the first thing expected to outgrow the ORM. The consolidated
answer belongs here once the pipeline exists.

## Known gaps

Everything in `docs/open-questions.md`, plus: no usage recording, no rating, no limits, no
invoicing, no reconciliation, and a load harness that measures latency but does not yet
verify a total.
